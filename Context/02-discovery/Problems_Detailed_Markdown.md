## Slide 4: Problem 1 --- Backstory

### Background

Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and technology have made
it easier for false information and fear-mongering content to spread.
The presentation highlights older generations as a group that may be
particularly vulnerable because some people have less experience with
technology and may find it harder to identify content that could have
been generated by AI.

### Main issue

People may encounter AI-generated material and mistake it for genuine
information. This can make misleading claims seem believable and
contribute to the spread of misinformation.

## Slide 5: Problem 1 --- Personal impact

The presentation explains that many of Jonathan's relatives regularly
fall victim to misinformation. This has begun to affect his day-to-day
life.

The slide also makes two important points:

-   The relatives mean well; the presentation does not blame them for
    believing misleading content.
-   The videos are described as "predatory by design," suggesting that
    the content is created or presented in a way that can take advantage
    of viewers' trust or difficulty identifying misinformation.

### Why this matters

The issue is not only about whether one person can identify false
information. Misinformation can affect families and everyday
interactions when people share content they believe to be true.

## Slide 6: Problem 1 --- Proposed solution: AI Validity Checker

### Proposed idea

**AI Validity Checker**

The proposed tool would authenticate or assess content to determine how
likely it is to have been generated by AI.

### Intended purpose

-   Help users assess whether content may be AI-generated.
-   Raise awareness that not everything people see online should
    automatically be believed.
-   Support more careful evaluation of potentially misleading content.

### Important distinction

The slide describes the tool as determining how likely content is to be
AI-generated. It does not claim that the tool can prove with complete
certainty whether every item is real or AI-generated.

## Slide 9: Problem 2 --- Real Example

The slide presents an example of people queuing for a concert where
there is no digital check-in system.

### Situation shown

People are waiting to enter an event, and the lack of a digital check-in
system is presented as part of the problem.

## Slide 10: Problem 2 --- Backstory

### Role and responsibilities

Luies explains that he worked as an usher or liaison officer (LO) at the
check-in table. His responsibility was to verify guests against the
guest list before allowing them to enter the venue.

### What happened

About 30 minutes before the venue opened, a large crowd arrived at once.
The existing guest-list setup could not handle the incoming guests
efficiently.

The presentation identifies several specific difficulties:

1.  Some guests were not yet included on the guest list.
2.  Some guests' names did not match the information on the list.
3.  Staff could check in only one person at a time.

### Core operational issue

The check-in process depended on a guest list that was difficult to
update or verify quickly enough during a sudden rush of arrivals.

## Slide 11: Problem 2 --- Consequences

The entrance became crowded, and the queue grew long. Guests became
frustrated while waiting.

The presentation states that the event started **35 minutes late**
because the staff could not process the crowd quickly enough.

### Effects of the problem

-   Congestion at the entrance.
-   Long waiting times.
-   Frustrated guests.
-   Delayed event start time.

These consequences show how a slow check-in process can affect both the
guest experience and the event schedule.

## Slide 12: Problem 2 --- Guiding question

The presentation asks:

> Is there a digital check-in system that would let ushers process
> guests faster?

### Problem statement

The central question is whether a digital system could help ushers
verify guests and manage entry more efficiently, particularly when many
people arrive at the same time.

### Desired outcome

The question points toward a check-in process that can handle guests
more quickly and reduce the delays and congestion described in the
backstory. The slide poses the question but does not specify a final
system design or a list of required features.

## Slide 16: Problem 3 --- Backstory

### The challenge of exercising alone

When people exercise alone, they need to pay attention to several
things, including:

-   Exercise programs.
-   Exercise form.
-   Running distance.
-   Repetitions.

Tracking these details helps people adjust their exercises and
understand their activity.

### Main issue

People who work out alone need to manage the exercise itself while also
remembering to record information about their performance.

## Slide 17: Problem 3 --- Consequences of poor tracking

The presentation explains that people often forget to record workout
information because they become tired and run out of breath while
exercising.

As a result:

-   Workout logs may be inaccurate.
-   Progress may be slower.
-   Motivation may decrease.

### Why tracking matters

If the information recorded after a workout is incomplete or inaccurate,
it may be harder for a person to understand their progress and make
appropriate adjustments to their exercise routine.

## Slide 18: Problem 3 --- Example of a personal trainer

The presenter recalls seeing an older man exercising with a personal
trainer.

During the workout:

-   The man focused on performing the exercises.
-   The trainer tracked his progress.
-   The trainer adjusted his exercise form to help prevent accidents and
    injuries.

### Lesson from the example

A personal trainer can take responsibility for tracking performance and
observing exercise form, allowing the person exercising to focus more
directly on the workout.

The example illustrates the kind of support that may be difficult for
someone exercising alone to access.

## Slide 19: Problem 3 --- Guiding question

The presentation asks:

> Wouldn't it be nice if everyone had easy access to a personal trainer
> that tracks their exercise, adjusts their workouts, and plans
> accordingly?

### Proposed direction

The question suggests a solution that could make personal-trainer-like
support more accessible.

The desired support would include:

1.  Tracking exercise activity and progress.
2.  Adjusting workouts based on the person's needs or performance.
3.  Planning workouts accordingly.

The slide frames this as an idea and does not provide technical
implementation details.

## Slide 21: Selected Problem --- Jonathan's AI Validity Checker

The presentation states that the group chose Jonathan's problem from the
problems discussed.

### Importance

As AI becomes more advanced, it is becoming more difficult to
distinguish real content from content generated by AI.

The proposed AI Validity Checker could help users identify potentially
false, AI-generated content. The presentation connects this need to the
difficulty of determining what is real and what is not.

### Feasibility and challenges

The presentation identifies three challenges:

1.  **Finding reliable indicators:** Finding specific, tell-tale aspects
    of content that are common across AI-generated content.
2.  **Reaching the target audience:** Delivering the tool to the
    intended audience.
3.  **Keeping up with AI content publishers:** Dealing with the ongoing
    challenge of AI-generated content publishers and the changing
    content they produce.

### Summary of the selection

The group selected the AI Validity Checker idea because identifying
potentially false AI-generated content is increasingly important.
However, building and delivering such a tool presents challenges,
especially in finding indicators that apply across different types of
AI-generated content.

# Overall Summary

The presentation discusses three everyday problems and then selects one
for further consideration.

## Problem 1: AI-generated misinformation

-   **Problem:** People, particularly those who are less comfortable
    with technology, may believe or share false information and
    AI-generated content.
-   **Personal impact:** Jonathan says that misinformation affects many
    of his relatives and has begun to affect his daily life.
-   **Proposed idea:** An AI Validity Checker that assesses how likely
    content is to be AI-generated.
-   **Main challenges:** Finding reliable indicators, reaching the
    target audience, and keeping up with AI content publishers.

## Problem 2: Slow event check-in

-   **Problem:** A manual guest-list process struggles when a large
    crowd arrives at once.
-   **Personal example:** Luies describes working as an usher/LO and
    checking guests against a list.
-   **Causes described:** Missing names, mismatched names, and checking
    in only one person at a time.
-   **Consequences:** A crowded entrance, long queues, frustrated
    guests, and a 35-minute event delay.
-   **Guiding question:** Could a digital check-in system help ushers
    process guests faster?

## Problem 3: Tracking exercise progress

-   **Problem:** People exercising alone may forget to record their
    workout details when they become tired or breathless.
-   **Consequences:** Inaccurate workout logs, slower progress, and
    reduced motivation.
-   **Example:** A personal trainer tracks an older man's progress and
    adjusts his form.
-   **Proposed direction:** Make personal-trainer-like tracking, workout
    adjustments, and planning more accessible.

## Final selection

The group chose **Jonathan's AI Validity Checker** problem. The
presentation emphasizes the growing difficulty of distinguishing real
content from AI-generated content and identifies reliability, audience
reach, and ongoing changes in AI-generated content as key challenges.
